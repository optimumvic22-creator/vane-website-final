## 1. What's wrong with the current page

The current page is well built and visually strong. Its problem is **what it says**:

- **It never says what the product is.** A coach leaves without knowing that MQS:
  - connects to their force plates;
  - compares their athletes to a global database;
  - analyses video from an iPhone;
  - writes the report with AI.
  The things that make MQS worth buying are missing.
- **It shows the score but never explains it.** The widget shows "MQS 55", but the page never says what the Movement Quality Score is, where it comes from or why it matters.
- **It doesn't say who it's for.** MQS makes high-level movement analysis easy for every coach, including coaches who have never touched a force plate. The page doesn't say so.
- **It repeats one idea five times.** "We interpret, you decide" appears in the hero, the three cards, "Beyond isolated tests", "Assess. Interpret. Apply." and "Your expertise stays essential". It's true and it's important, but it belongs in one section, not five.
- **It talks about features and process, not about the coach's gain.** "One standardized assessment", "same protocol at baseline and retest" and "one shared report" describe our method. A coach wants to know what they get:
  - better decisions;
  - comparison with the world;
  - hours back every week.
- **It's defensive.** Two of the eight sections mostly say what MQS *doesn't* do (no diagnosis, no decisions for you). Keep that once, short, near the end.
- **It shows almost nothing.** The only product visual is the score widget at the top. There are no screenshots of analysis, video, the report or the force-plate sync.
- **"Still in development" appears three times.** Remove it everywhere. The waitlist section only says that we'll contact people when their access opens.

**Keep:**
- the visual style (dark, condensed uppercase headlines, cyan accent);
- the interactive 7-domain score widget, which moves to the new "Introducing the Movement Quality Score" section;
- the "not a medical diagnosis" line;
- the waitlist form and its consent wording.

## 2. The story the page must tell (in this order)

One sentence: **MQS makes objective movement analysis easy. One unified assessment gives every athlete a Movement Quality Score, the common language that connects every coach on MQS. NormVault compares the score and every underlying force-plate and kinematic data point with athletes worldwide, and a clear report tells you which qualities to train. It's high-level analysis without the years it normally takes to learn it.**

### The main angle: we make you proficient, fast

This is the thread that runs through the whole page:

> **Objective movement analysis used to be hard to get into.** You needed expensive equipment, years of experience with force plates and kinematics, and hours per athlete. **MQS removes that hardship.** It helps every coach become proficient at objective movement analysis: at a high level, quickly, with little effort.

Every section should leave the visitor thinking "I could do this, and do it well, starting next week."

### The core of the product: the Movement Quality Score

Everything on the page connects back to the score:

> **The unified MQS assessment → the Movement Quality Score → NormVault.** Every coach assesses the same way, so every score means the same thing everywhere. That shared standard is what NormVault is built on, and what connects every coach, athlete and facility on MQS.

### The coaching idea underneath

This is Dario's coaching philosophy, and the page should land it clearly:

> **If you front-load the work into the needs analysis, exercise selection becomes the easy part. The needs tell you what to pick.**

Most coaches struggle with "what do I train, which exercises do I pick?" The honest answer is that this is a needs-analysis problem, not an exercise problem. MQS does the needs analysis, and the coach does the programming. Section 3.2 is built on this idea.

### The steps, each one a benefit to the coach

| # | The coach's problem | What MQS gives them |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Movement analysis feels like a field for specialists. Where do I even start?" | **Made easy**: high-level analysis from day one, without years of learning. |
| 2 | "Every test gives me a different number. What's the overall picture?" | **The Movement Quality Score**: one unified assessment, seven domains, one score that means the same thing everywhere. |
| 3 | "I have data but no reference. Is 38 cm good for this athlete?" | **NormVault**: compare the score *and every single force-plate and kinematic data point* with athletes worldwide. |

| 9 | "Will this replace me?" | **No. You make every training decision.** MQS buys back your time for the work only a coach can do. |

### 3.12 FAQ `#faq`

Use an accordion. Answers are short on purpose.

- **What is the Movement Quality Score?**
  One score for how well someone moves, built from a unified assessment across seven domains: gait, postural control, force capacity, power, motor control, neuro response and dual-task cost. Every coach assesses the same way, so every score means the same thing everywhere.
- **I've never done movement analysis. Is MQS for me?**
  Yes. That's exactly who it's built for. MQS guides you through the tests, sorts the data and explains every result, so you work at a high level from your first assessment.
- **What can I compare in NormVault?**
  Everything: the overall MQS, each of the seven domains, and every single force-plate metric and kinematic variable.
- **Which force plates work with MQS?**
  Hawkin Dynamics, VALD and CC Athletics connect through your account. Lab plates such as Bertec connect directly to the desktop app. Other data can be imported as files.
- **Do I need a force plate?**
  No. Every coach starts with an iPhone: you get kinematic variables, jump height from flight time and kinetics from video, calibrated in our lab. A force plate adds the full force-time analysis.
- **How many iPhones do I need?**
  One is enough to start. Two or three give you more angles and better data.
- **Can I assess athletes remotely?**
  Yes. Your athlete films a standardized assessment and sends you the videos. You upload them to the MQS desktop app and get the full analysis.
- **Does the AI decide the training?**
  No. MQS tells you which qualities need training. You choose the exercises and build the plan.
- **Who can see my athletes' data?**
  Only you and the people you invite. NormVault receives anonymised numbers only, never names or video.
- **Mac or Windows?**
  Both. The MQS Capture app runs on iPhone.
- **Is MQS a medical device?**
  No. MQS gives movement information for training decisions, not a medical diagnosis.
- **What does it cost?**
  We'll share pricing with everyone on the waitlist first.

## 5. Before publishing: one short check

Product features are written as finished; that's settled. Three things are about advertising law, not the product, and need a quick look before the page goes live:

1. **Superlatives must be provable.** In the EU, "the largest" and "the best" have to hold up against competitors. That's why the copy says "*built to become* the largest movement-quality database" and why headline A is recommended in 3.7. ## 7. Style rules for the copy

- **Easy, fast, high-level.** The visitor should feel that objective movement analysis is now within reach, that MQS helps them get good at it fast, and that it takes little effort. Avoid anything that makes it sound complicated or academic.
- **The score is the anchor.** Name it in full ("Movement Quality Score") the first time in a section, then "MQS" or "the score". NormVault, the report and the setups all connect back to it.
- **MQS has two meanings; keep them clear.** "MQS" is the software, and "the Movement Quality Score" is the score. When it could be ambiguous, write out "the Movement Quality Score".
- **Benefit first, feature second.** Every section starts with what the coach gains. The feature that delivers it comes next.
- **Present tense, finished product.** No "coming soon", "planned", "in development" or "will".
- **Company vs. product:** **VANE Science** is the company. On this page, say "MQS" (not "VANE interprets…").
- **Say "coach" and "athlete"**, not "user" or "client" (physios are covered in the eyebrow).
- **The coach stays in charge.** Say this clearly once, in 3.10, and briefly in the hero subline ("You make the call"). Don't repeat it in every section.
- **Words to avoid:** "diagnosis", "AI decides", "AI coach", "replaces", "the largest" / "the best" (see section 5), "100 % accurate", and any person's name for the expertise behind the AI.
- **Keep sentences short.** One idea per sentence. Headlines in the site's uppercase style; body text in normal sentence case.
- **No invented numbers.** No counters, athlete totals or percentages unless they're real.
